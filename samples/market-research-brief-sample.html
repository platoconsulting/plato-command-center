Market Research Brief: Opening a Specialty Coffee Shop in Round Rock, TX

Prepared for Ember & Oak Coffee (a fictional client used for this sample) · Plato Consulting LLC · November 2026 · Standard package
The question: Is there room in Round Rock for one more independent specialty coffee shop, and if so, how should it compete?

1. Bottom line

  • Yes, with conditions. The city has grown about 20% since the 2020 Census, and specialty coffee is now the most common way American adults drink coffee.
  • The real competition is the daily habit, not other cafes. Fast drive-through chains own the morning run. An independent wins by being the best place to sit and fast for mobile orders.
  • Next step: test a site in a growth corridor with few independents, and run a pop-up to test prices before signing a lease.

What this means: the market is crowded at both ends. Craft-focused independents own "best coffee," and drive-through chains own "fastest coffee." The open space is a shop that does both: real seating plus a fast mobile pickup lane, in a part of town the independents have not reached yet.

6. Trends to build around

  • Specialty is now the default. Past-day specialty drinking passed traditional coffee for the first time in 2025 [5].
  • Cold drinks drive summer. The NCA's 2025 summer market report found cold coffee dominated the season [11].
  • Ordering is moving to phones. App-based coffee orders reached a record high in 2025 [6].
  • Drive-through chains are spreading in the suburbs. 7 Brew, Dutch Bros, and Black Rock are all present in Round Rock (our observation from the listings above).

7. Risks and unknowns

  • Rent and buildout are not covered here. They depend on the site and need real quotes.
  • Price pressure from drive-through chains on the commuter segment.
  • Population figures disagree. The city estimate is about 6% above the Census Bureau's 2024 survey figure. If the lower figure is closer, the market in section 3 shrinks by about 5%.
  • Bean costs have been volatile. Plan for menu price changes during the first year.

8. Recommendations

  1. Choose a hybrid format: 30 or more seats plus a dedicated mobile pickup shelf, or a drive-up window if the site allows it.
  2. Launch with mobile ordering and a simple loyalty program. The chains already train customers to expect both.
  3. Differentiate on the menu: a strong cold and seasonal lineup, plus a local roaster partnership for credibility.
  4. Test before you sign: run a four-weekend pop-up to test prices in the $5.50 to $7.00 range and see real demand.
  5. Next research step: a short intercept survey (about 150 people) and foot-traffic data for two or three candidate sites.
